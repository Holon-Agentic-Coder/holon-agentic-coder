### Strategy Rationale

**What went wrong on PR #258 (confirmed in planning).** The report evaluated `aad504e`; the head became `a4afb30`, a
merge of `main` whose only conflicts were ledger files. `head_move_since_calibration` lists `evaluated..head` over all
parents, so it counted the merge commit and the squash commit of `main` reachable through the merge's second parent: the
"2 non-calibration commit(s)". The warning was posted at 09:18:09Z by the run whose Stage 5 rerun pushed the fresh
report at 09:18:34Z. The superseded edit happens in `rerun_calibration` only after `run_stage` returns, and `run_stage`
includes the final-head CI wait, so the edit came at about 09:29Z, after the PR had merged at 09:23:07Z.

**Design, in the order of the steps.**

1. Staleness is judged on the PR's own change. Commits are listed first-parent only, bookkeeping commits (calibration
   commits and ledger-only commits) are discounted, and anything left is settled by comparing the PR-own fingerprint of
   the evaluated commit with that of the head. Equal fingerprints mean the report still covers the change.
2. The warning is posted only when the stale report is going to stay. A run that reruns Stage 5 posts nothing first; it
   posts once only when the rerun fails or the head outruns every attempt.
3. Stage 5 edits an open warning to superseded right after `publish_calibration` succeeds and before
   `check_final_head_ci`, naming the evaluated SHA of the report it just pushed. The refreshed note is not posted when a
   warning was superseded, so a warning followed by a fresh report leaves exactly one comment.
4. The warning text says what the flow does (it reruns Stage 5) and no longer instructs a human.
