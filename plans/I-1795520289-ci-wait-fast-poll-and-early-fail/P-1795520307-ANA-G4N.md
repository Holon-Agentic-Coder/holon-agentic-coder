### Strategy Rationale

The intent is accepted as-is because it delivers clear latency reduction across every flow (two to three CI waits per
PR, eliminating up to ~105 seconds of idle polling tail per wait and removing unnecessary waiting after a confirmed
failure) at very low entropy (`2.80`) and low cost (`12.0`).

Code inspection revealed three concrete facts that shape the plan:

1. `sandbox_executor/flow/github/ci_wait.py` defines `CI_WAIT_POLL_CEILING_SECONDS = 120.0`, which is the default
   `ceiling` on `CiWaitPolicy` used by the review-loop (`loop.py`), merged-head (`merged_head_gate.py`), and final-head
   (`ci_final_head.py`) waits. Lowering it to `15.0` and clamping initial `interval` to `policy.ceiling` (when
   `policy.ceiling > 0`) caps polling at 15 seconds across all three callers while preserving exponential backoff via
   `next_poll_interval` when a caller starts below `15.0`.
2. Fail-early on the first failed check is already active in review-loop (`wait_for_pending_ci` defaults to
   `stop_on_failure=True`) and merged-head (`classify_head_checks` returns `FAILED` before checking `PENDING`), whereas
   `ci_final_head.py` explicitly overrides `stop_on_failure=False`. Removing that override makes fail-early uniform
   across all three wait paths. `ci_reverify.py` performs pure verdict mapping without polling and already maps
   `state == "failed"` to `RED`, so it needs no code edits.
3. Updating `ci_final_head.py` to fail early intentionally reverses the old assertion in
   `tests/test_calibrate_final_head_ci.py` (`test_failure_does_not_end_the_wait_while_other_checks_are_pending`), and
   lowering the ceiling to `15.0` updates sleep-sequence assertions in `tests/test_review_ci_wait.py`. Both test updates
