### Strategy Rationale

The intent is small (469 source lines, 36 findings), fully scoped and has a direct precedent, so it is accepted as is
and executed as one plan without sub-intents. Test first (Step 1) so the before count is recorded by a failing test,
then clean the runners package (Step 2) and the launcher/CLI pair (Step 3), then verify once (Step 4).

Aggregation: `p_success` is the product-style bottleneck judgement, slightly above the plain product of steps
(`0.93 * 0.94 * 0.90 * 0.90 = 0.71`) because the steps are strongly correlated (the same executor, same pattern; a
failure in Step 4 is fixed in place, not fatal), so `0.86` is used. Entropy is the sum of step entropies
(`0.8 + 0.8 + 1.0 + 0.4 = 3.0`). Cost is the sum of step costs (`2.5 + 2.0 + 2.5 + 2.5 = 9.5`). Impact is a qualitative
judgement of the whole slice (70: one more package made true to the self-explaining rule, with a guard test that stops
regressions). Learning value is the max-plus-small-increment of the steps (3.0).

`EV = P(success) * Impact + mu * LearningValue - lambda * Delta_S_intent - Cost`
`EV = 0.86 * 70.0 + 0.5 * 3.0 - 0.3 * 3.0 - 9.5 = 60.20 + 1.50 - 0.90 - 9.50 = 51.30`

Bottleneck step: Step 3 (`launcher/run_tracking.py`), because it holds 16 of the 36 findings, its fields
(`container_names`, `clients`) are read by `flow/process_reaper.py` and cannot be renamed, and several comments carry
real reasons (signal-handler re-entrancy, SIGTTIN, `--rm`) that must survive as `# why:` comments rather than be lost.

Strong assumptions: the shared `tests/self_doc_scanner.py` stays untouched (it is out of scope) and does not accept
